## What we counted

We took every active listing with an AI or machine learning term in the title (797 of them, from 39 companies) and checked each job description for specific words. A mention counts whether the skill is required or only "nice to have", and we count a listing once per skill no matter how many times it's mentioned. How the listings are collected and labeled is explained in [how we collect and label job listings](post:how-we-collect-and-label-job-listings).

## What stands out

**Python is the common language, but not universal.** At 58%, it leads the list by a wide margin. The other 42% includes jobs with little coding, such as recruiting or sales roles that happen to have "AI" in the title (more on that below).

**LLMs are in nearly half of AI job descriptions.** At 45%, "large language model" or "LLM" appears in more descriptions than PyTorch, SQL or any cloud provider. Fine-tuning (15%), reinforcement learning or RLHF (13%) and RAG (8%) follow. These are narrower specialties, so smaller shares are expected.

**PyTorch is the framework to know.** It appears in 207 listings versus 97 for TensorFlow, about 2.1 times as often. JAX appears in 81, close to TensorFlow.

**Systems skills matter more than you might expect.** "Distributed" appears in 254 listings (32%), more than PyTorch, and C++ (16%) is mentioned slightly more often than SQL (14%). Training and serving large models involve a lot of systems work, and the descriptions reflect that.

**Cloud skills are spread evenly.** AWS (12%), Google Cloud (10%) and Azure (9%) are close, which suggests employers rarely favor one provider, and the concepts carry across clouds. Kubernetes (10%) and Spark (9%) appear at similar rates.

**GPU and low-level skills are rarer.** CUDA (6%) and Rust (3%) are specialized, and likely appear mostly in infrastructure-heavy roles.

## What to take from it

If you are building skills for an AI career, this data is consistent with a sensible order of priority:
1. **Python, plus enough PyTorch to build and train models.**
2. **Working knowledge of how LLMs are trained and used:** fine-tuning, evaluation and retrieval.
3. **Systems fundamentals:** distributed computing and how to run workloads on a cloud and on GPUs.
4. **Specializations** (CUDA, JAX, reinforcement learning) once you know which area you want.

That is a reading of one dataset, not a guarantee. Always match your skills to the specific job description. ## What this data can't tell you

- **Mentions aren't requirements.** A skill may appear in a "nice to have" line or in a company description.
- **Not every AI-titled listing is an engineering job.** Our AI label is based on the title, so roles such as technical recruiter or account executive with "AI" in the title are included. That lowers the percentages for engineering skills compared with how common they are in engineering roles alone.
- **Keyword matching is blunt.** We searched for specific words. A description could describe a skill without using our exact term, and a short word can appear in an unrelated sense.
- **We left out the word "evaluation."** It appeared in 56% of listings, but it is too general (it appears in performance-review language too) to say much about machine learning skills.
- **It's 39 companies,** so smaller employers may ask for different things.
