**Short answer:** Almost nobody hires for a job called "prompt engineer." **Only 1 of 6,138 active listings on {{SITE_NAME}} has "prompt" in its title** (late October 2026), and it is a software engineering role at Anthropic called "Software Engineer, Tokens and Prompt Structures." But the skill appears everywhere: **153 distinct roles (about 3%) mention prompt engineering, prompt design or prompting in the description**, and 33 of them carry an AI label. Prompting shows up as a skill inside other jobs, not as a job of its own.

## What we measured

We searched titles for "prompt" and searched descriptions for "prompt engineering", "prompt design" and "prompting". Roles open in several cities are counted once. This is a snapshot of 39 companies, mostly well-funded AI and tech employers, so it says nothing about smaller firms or freelance work.

## The roles that mention it

The most common titles tell the story:

- **Engineering and customer-facing roles:** "Forward Deployed Engineer" (4 roles), "Software Engineer, Agent" and "Staff Software Engineer, Agents" (2 each), "Engineering Manager, Agent" (2), "Applied AI Engineer, Enterprise" (2) and "GTM Engineer" (3). For more on the first, see [forward deployed engineer jobs](post:forward-deployed-engineer-jobs-who-hires-and-what-they-ask-for).
- **Domain experts working with AI:** at Harvey, "Legal Engineer" roles (three variants, six roles in total) and "Applied Legal Researcher" (2) mention prompting.
- **Strategy roles:** "Strategist, Agent Development" (2 roles, in a Spanish-speaking version).

So the skill appears in engineering, in customer-facing technical work, and in roles where someone with expertise in a field (law, in these examples) works with an AI system. In none of these is prompting the whole job.

## What this means if you want to work with prompts

- **Treat prompting as an add-on skill.** The postings pair it with engineering, customer work or domain expertise, not with a standalone title.
- **Search for the role, not the skill.** Titles such as "agent engineer," "applied AI engineer," "forward deployed engineer" and "legal engineer" are where the descriptions mention it.
- **Look at who hires.** Roughly three in five of the mentions come from Sierra, Anthropic and Harvey.
- **Pair it with another strength.** For the skills that appear most often across AI roles, see [what AI job descriptions ask for](post:skills-ai-job-descriptions-ask-for), where Python, LLMs and distributed systems lead.

## What this data can't tell you

- **A mention isn't a duty.** Some descriptions mention prompting while describing the company's product, not the job.
- **Other employers may use the title.** Smaller companies, agencies and freelance platforms may advertise "prompt engineer" roles that we don't see.
- **"Prompting" can mean other things,** so a few mentions may not be about AI.
- It is a snapshot from late October 2026.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is prompt engineer a real job?**
As a job title, it is very rare in our data (1 title out of 6,138 listings, and that one is a software engineering role). As a skill, it appears in about 3% of roles.

**Which roles use prompt engineering?**
Engineering and applied AI roles, forward deployed engineers, agent engineers, and domain-expert roles such as legal engineers.

**Should I learn prompt engineering?**
It shows up as a useful skill alongside other strengths, so learning it makes sense as part of a broader skill set, not as a career on its own.

**Which companies mention it most?**
Sierra (50 roles), Anthropic (28) and Harvey (16).
