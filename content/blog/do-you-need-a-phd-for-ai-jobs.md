**Short answer:** Most AI job descriptions don't mention a PhD. Among the **688 roles labeled AI** on {{SITE_NAME}} in late October 2026, **145 (21%) mention a PhD or doctorate**, 114 (17%) mention a master's degree, 155 (23%) mention a bachelor's degree, and 138 (20%) include "or equivalent" experience wording. That means about four in five AI job descriptions never mention a PhD. A mention is not a requirement, though, because a description may say "preferred" or list a PhD among several options.

## What we measured

We took the AI-labeled roles (a title with an AI or machine learning term, excluding recruiting, sales, finance, legal and similar functions) and counted how many descriptions contain each phrase. We counted a role once even if it is open in several cities. The keyword searches look for "PhD", "Ph.D." and "doctorate", for master's and bachelor's wording, and for "or equivalent" or "equivalent experience".

## What stands out

**A PhD is mentioned in about one description in five.** That is roughly as often as a bachelor's degree, and no degree level is mentioned in more than 23% of descriptions. Many descriptions list skills and experience without naming a degree at all.

**Equivalent-experience wording is common.** About one in five descriptions says a degree can be replaced by equivalent experience. 36 of the 145 PhD mentions appear alongside that kind of wording. We can't tell from this data whether the two phrases are attached to each other, since "or equivalent" may refer to a different degree level, but it shows that many employers leave room for experience.

**Most AI jobs are engineering jobs, not research jobs.** In our October 20 title counts, there were 124 machine learning engineer listings and 147 AI engineer listings, against 113 research scientist or research engineer listings. Research roles also pay more in our [salary analysis](post:ai-machine-learning-salary-ranges-from-job-listings) (a median midpoint of about $370K, against $276K to $285K for machine learning and AI engineering). We did not split the PhD mentions by role type, so we can't say how much of the 21% sits in research roles. That is a reasonable guess, but not something this data shows.

## How to read a description

- **Look for the wording around the degree.** "PhD required" and "PhD preferred" are different. A mention in a list of "nice to have" items is a weaker signal.
- **Check for equivalent-experience language.** One in five descriptions includes it.
- **Match the role to your path.** If you aim at research roles, expect a doctorate to come up more often. If you aim at engineering roles, the skills in the description matter more. See [what AI job descriptions ask for](post:skills-ai-job-descriptions-ask-for).
- **Look at early-career routes.** Some employers run programs aimed at PhD students or new PhD graduates, and others hire new graduates with a bachelor's or master's. See [entry-level and internship roles](post:entry-level-and-internship-ai-tech-jobs-who-is-hiring).

## What this data can't tell you

- **A mention is not a requirement.** We counted words, not whether the employer requires, prefers or merely mentions a degree.
- **The master's count can include stray matches,** such as unrelated uses of "MS".
- **The 688 roles come from 39 companies,** mostly well-funded AI employers, and don't represent every employer.
- **We can't see who gets hired.** Descriptions describe what employers ask for, not who they select.
- It is a snapshot from late October 2026.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a PhD to work in AI?**
Most listings don't mention one: 79% of the AI-labeled roles we looked at never mention a PhD or doctorate. Some roles, especially research roles, probably ask for one more often, but this data doesn't split them out.

**Is a master's degree enough?**
A master's degree is mentioned in 17% of descriptions. Many roles mention other degree levels or none, so check the specific role.

**Can experience replace a degree?**
About 20% of descriptions include "or equivalent" experience wording, so some employers say it can.

**How current is this?**
It is from late October 2026. Employers open and close roles every week.
